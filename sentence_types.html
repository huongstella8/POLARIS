🗺️ Types of Sentences

Four structures every writer needs to master

Every English sentence belongs to one of four structural types. Understanding them helps you write more varied, sophisticated, and clear prose.

📊 The Four Sentence Types

TypeStructureExample
Simple1 independent clauseGrammar is easy.
Compound2+ independent clauses (coordinator / conjunctive adverb / semicolon)Grammar is easy, so I learned it quickly.
Complex1 independent + 1+ dependent clause (subordinator)Because grammar is easy, I learned it quickly.
Compound-Complex2+ independent + 1+ dependent clauseBecause grammar is easy, I learned it quickly, but it took years to master writing.

🔑 Key Terms

  • Independent clause — has a subject + verb and can stand alone as a sentence: The sun rises.
  • Dependent clause — has a subject + verb but CANNOT stand alone; needs an independent clause: Because the sun rises…
  • Coordinator (FANBOYS) — joins two independent clauses: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so
  • Subordinator — begins a dependent clause: because, although, when, if, that, who…
  • Conjunctive adverb — connects two independent clauses with a semicolon: ; therefore, ; however, ; moreover,
💡 Câu đơn (Simple) có thể có nhiều chủ ngữ và/hoặc nhiều động từ nhưng chỉ có MỘT mệnh đề độc lập.
Freshwater boils and freezes at different temperatures. → vẫn là câu đơn (1 S, 2 V).

1️⃣ Simple Sentence

One independent clause — the foundation of all writing

✦ Definition

A simple sentence is one independent clause. It has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought.

📐 Structure Variations

PatternExample
1 Subject + 1 VerbFreshwater boils at 100°C at sea level.
1 Subject + 2 VerbsFreshwater boils at 100°C and freezes at 0°C.
2 Subjects + 2 VerbsFreshwater and salt water do not boil and do not freeze at the same temperatures.
Still ONE independent clause — no dependent clause, no second main clause.

✍️ Writing with Variety

  • Too many short simple sentences make writing choppy: The hotel is nice. It has good views. The food is good.
  • Vary by using precise, descriptive vocabulary instead of repeated words:
    nice → secluded, stylish, spacious | good → breathtaking, delicious, relaxing
  • Simple sentences work best for emphasis or clarity — especially after a longer sentence.
💡 Lưu ý: Câu đơn có thể rất dài với nhiều cụm từ bổ nghĩa, nhưng vẫn chỉ có một mệnh đề chính.
The tall young Vietnamese woman with black hair and a bright smile walked slowly down the narrow street. → Simple sentence (1 S + 1 V)

🔗 Compound Sentence – FANBOYS

Joining two independent clauses with coordinating conjunctions

✦ Method 1: Coordinating Conjunctions

Independent Clause , coordinator Independent Clause

🎯 FANBOYS — The 7 Coordinators

WordFunctionExample
ForTo add a reason (= because)Japanese people live longer, for they eat healthful diets.
AndTo add a similar, equal ideaThey eat a lot of fish, and they eat lightly.
NorTo add a negative equal ideaThey do not eat red meat, nor do they eat dairy products.
ButTo add an opposite ideaDiet is one factor, but it is not the only one.
OrTo add an alternative possibilityLimit animal fat, or you risk heart disease.
YetTo add an unexpected continuationSmoking is a risk factor, yet many Asians have high tobacco use.
SoTo add an expected resultStress shortens life, so try to avoid it.

📌 Punctuation Rule

  • Always use a comma before a coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses.
  • Salt water boils at a higher temperature, so food cooks faster.
  • but vs yet: Both show contrast/surprise. Yet implies more surprise than but.
  • nor: After nor, use inverted word order: …nor do they eat dairy.
💡 Mẹo nhớ: FANBOYS = For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So
Luôn đặt dấu phẩy TRƯỚC coordinator khi nối hai mệnh đề độc lập.
✗ She studied hard but she failed. → ✓ She studied hard, but she failed.

⚙️ Compound – Conjunctive Adverbs

Connecting independent clauses with a semicolon + conjunctive adverb

✦ Method 2: Conjunctive Adverbs

Independent Clause ; conjunctive adverb, Independent Clause

Community colleges offer job preparation; also, they prepare students for four-year colleges.

📋 Complete Reference Table

FunctionConjunctive Adverbs
To add informationalso, besides, furthermore, in addition, moreover
To add a resultas a result, consequently, therefore, thus
To show contrast / concessionhowever, nevertheless, nonetheless, on the other hand
To show "first is false, second is true"instead, on the contrary, rather
To show "as a substitute"instead (of doing A, do B)
To give another possibilityalternatively, on the other hand, otherwise
To add explanationin other words, that is
To make a stronger statementindeed, in fact
To list in time ordermeanwhile, afterward, then, subsequently
To give an examplefor example, for instance
To show similaritysimilarly, likewise

📌 Punctuation Rule

  • Use a semicolon (;) before and a comma (,) after the conjunctive adverb.
  • Native English speakers have different needs; as a result, schools provide separate classes.
  • however = most common; shows unexpected contrast.
  • otherwise = "if not" (condition/consequence).
💡 Phân biệt:
however (tuy nhiên) = unexpected contrast after a positive statement.
on the other hand = giving an alternative option or perspective.
on the contrary / instead = the first statement is wrong; here is the correct one.
otherwise = if not (implies condition): Study hard; otherwise, you will fail.

； Compound – Semicolon

Joining closely related independent clauses with a semicolon alone

✦ Method 3: Semicolon Alone

Independent Clause ; Independent Clause

Poland was the first Eastern European country to turn away from communism; others soon followed.

📌 When to Use

  • Use a semicolon alone when the two clauses are closely related and the relationship is obvious without a connector.
  • This is the most formal of the three compound methods.
  • The clauses should be roughly equal in importance.
  • A capital letter does NOT follow a semicolon (unless it is a proper noun).
Grammar is easy; I learned it quickly. (the connection is clear — result)
Grammar is easy; therefore, I learned it quickly. (conjunctive adverb makes it explicit)
Grammar is easy, so I learned it quickly. (coordinator)
💡 Ba cách nối câu ghép — Tổng hợp:
① , + coordinator: …easy, so I learned…
② ; + conjunctive adverb + ,: …easy; therefore, I learned…
③ ; alone: …easy; I learned…

⏰ Complex – Adverb Clauses

Subordinating conjunctions for time, place, reason, purpose, result, condition, and contrast

✦ Complex Sentence with Adverb Clause

A dependent adverb clause modifies the main verb (tells when, where, why, how, under what condition, etc.)

Although women could own property, they could not vote until 1920. → [dependent clause first → comma]
A citizen can vote when he or she is 18. → [dependent clause last → no comma usually]

⏱️ Time (When?)

SubordinatorExample
afterAfter we ate lunch, we decided to go shopping.
as / just asJust as we left the house, it started to rain.
as long asWe waited as long as we could.
as soon asAs soon as the front door closed, I looked for my key.
beforeI thought I had put it in my pocket before we left.
sinceI have not locked myself out since I was 10 years old.
untilUntil I was 12, my mother pinned the key to my coat.
whenWhen I turned 12, my mother let me keep the key.
wheneverI put the key in the same place whenever I come home.
whileWhile I searched for the key, it rained harder.

📍 Place | 🎭 Manner | 📏 Distance | 🔄 Frequency

CategorySubordinatorsExample
Placewhere, wherever, anywhere, everywhereI like to shop where prices are low.
Manneras / just as, as if, as thoughYou look as if you didn't sleep.
Distanceas + adverb + asWe will hike as far as we can.
Frequencyas often asI call my parents as often as I can.

❓ Reason | 🎯 Purpose | ⚡ Result

CategorySubordinatorsExample
Reason (Why?)because, since, asI can't attend because I work at night.
Purposeso that, in order thatPeople emigrate so that their children can have a better life.
Resultso + adj/adv + that; such a(n) + noun + thatI was so tired that I fell asleep at dinner.

🔀 Condition | ↔️ Contrast & Concession

CategorySubordinatorsExample
Conditionif, unlessWe will not go hiking unless the weather is perfect.
Partial contrast (concession)although, even though, thoughI love my brother although we disagree about almost everything.
Direct contrastwhile, whereasMy brother likes classical music, while I prefer hard rock.
💡 Quy tắc dấu câu:
✓ Dependent clause TRƯỚC → dùng dấu phẩy: Because it rained, we stayed home.
✓ Dependent clause SAU → thường không dấu phẩy: We stayed home because it rained.
✗ Ngoại lệ: although / even though / whereas / while ở cuối → VẪN dùng dấu phẩy khi đảo vị trí.

🎯 Complex – Adjective Clauses

Relative clauses that describe nouns: who, whom, whose, which, that, when, where

✦ Adjective Clause = Relative Clause

A dependent adjective clause follows and modifies a noun. It answers: Which one? What kind?

Men who are not married are called bachelors. → [clause identifies which men]
Last year we vacationed in Cozumel, which features excellent scuba diving. → [clause adds info about Cozumel]

📋 Relative Pronouns

To refer toPronounExample
People (subject)who, that (informal)People who live in glass houses…
People (object)whom, that (informal)The man whom my sister married…
People (possessive)whoseA child whose parents are dead is called an orphan.
Animals & thingswhich, thatMy computer, which I bought yesterday, stopped working.
TimewhenThanksgiving is a time when families travel far.
PlacewhereAn orphanage is a place where orphans live.

📌 Defining vs Non-defining

  • Defining (restrictive) — identifies which one; NO commas:
    The book that I borrowed was excellent. (specifies which book)
  • Non-defining (non-restrictive) — adds extra info; USE commas; use which (NOT that):
    This book, which I borrowed from the library, was excellent.
  • that can only be used in defining clauses (not after commas).
💡 Khi nào bỏ được đại từ quan hệ?
Có thể bỏ who, whom, that, which khi đó là tân ngữ của mệnh đề quan hệ:
✓ The book [that] I read was great. (I read the book → "that" là tân ngữ → có thể bỏ)
✗ Không được bỏ khi là chủ ngữ: The book that was on the table…

💬 Complex – Noun Clauses

Dependent clauses that function as nouns: that, whether/if, wh-words

✦ Noun Clause = Subject or Object

[That there is a hole in the ozone layer] is well known. → noun clause as subject
Scientists know [what caused it]. → noun clause as object

📋 Noun Clause Connectors

TypeWordsExample
Statement contentthatShe knows that grammar is important.
Yes/No question contentwhether, if, whether or notI don't know whether I locked the door.
Do you know if the bus has arrived?
Wh-question contentwho, what, where, when, why, how, whichWe should ask when the bus arrives.
Do you know where the station is?
Degree/quantityhow much, how many, how long, how farHe didn't care how long he had to wait.

📌 Word Order in Noun Clauses

  • Noun clauses always use statement word order (Subject + Verb), NOT question word order.
  • ✗ Do you know where is the station?
  • ✓ Do you know where the station is?
  • ✗ I wonder what does he want.
  • ✓ I wonder what he wants.
💡 Phân biệt "if" và "whether":
if = thường dùng sau động từ; không dùng làm chủ ngữ.
whether = dùng được ở cả đầu câu và cuối câu; chính thức hơn.
✓ Whether he comes or not doesn't matter. (subject → phải dùng whether)
✓ I don't know if/whether he is coming.

🔀 Compound-Complex Sentence

Two or more independent clauses + one or more dependent clauses

✦ Definition

A compound-complex sentence has two or more independent clauses AND one or more dependent clauses. It combines both compound and complex structures.

🔧 Formation Examples

① I wanted to travel after I graduated from college; however, I had to go to work immediately.
[Indep. + dep. clause] ; [conjunctive adverb + indep.]
② After I graduated from college, I wanted to travel, but I had to go to work immediately.
[dep. clause,] [indep.] , but [indep.]
③ I wanted to travel after I graduated from college, but I had to go to work immediately because I had to support my family.
[indep. + dep.], but [indep. + dep.]
④ I could not decide where I should work or what I should do, so at first I did nothing.
[indep. + 2 noun clauses], so [indep.]

📌 Key Rules

  • Must have at least 2 independent clauses (compound) AND at least 1 dependent clause (complex).
  • The two independent clauses are joined by a coordinator, conjunctive adverb, or semicolon.
  • The dependent clause can be adverb, adjective, or noun clause.
  • All the punctuation rules from compound AND complex sentences apply.
💡 Cách nhận biết:
Tìm: ① ít nhất 2 mệnh đề độc lập (có thể đứng một mình) + ② ít nhất 1 mệnh đề phụ (bắt đầu bằng subordinating conjunction, relative pronoun hoặc that/whether/wh-word).
Câu compound-complex thường dài và phức tạp hơn — lý tưởng cho văn viết học thuật.
