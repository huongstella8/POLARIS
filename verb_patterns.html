What Are Verb Patterns?

Every English verb that is followed by another verb has a fixed pattern — you must use either to-infinitive, V-ing (gerund), or bare infinitive. There are no shortcuts; you must memorise which form each verb requires.

enjoy + swimming ✓    enjoy + to swim ✗
want + to swim ✓    want + swimming ✗
can + swim (bare) ✓    can + to swim ✗

💡 Gerund = noun: The V-ing form acts as a noun here. "I enjoy swimming" = "I enjoy it." You can test this by replacing the gerund with a pronoun.

Pattern: Verb + to-inf OR V-ing (No meaning change)

These verbs take EITHER form — meaning is the same:
"She loves cooking." = "She loves to cook."
"It started raining." = "It started to rain."

Special Group: Bare Infinitive (no 'to')

make / let / have + obj + bare infinitive
"She made him apologize."   "Let me help you."
❌ Common error: "She made him to apologize." ✗ — no 'to' after make/let/have!

Perception verbs: see/hear/watch/feel + obj

+ bare inf (complete action) OR + V-ing (action in progress)
"I saw her cross the road." (complete)   "I saw her crossing the road." (in progress)

Pattern: Verb/Adjective + Preposition + V-ing

After a preposition, always use V-ing (never to-inf)
"She is good at speaking French." ✓
"She is good at to speak French." ✗
⚠️ KEY TRAP — "look forward to": The 'to' here is a PREPOSITION, not an infinitive marker!
"I look forward to seeing you." ✓    "I look forward to see you." ✗

Verbs with Meaning Change

These verbs change meaning depending on which form follows. Both are grammatically correct, but they mean different things.

Verb+ to-infinitive+ V-ing
stoppause in order to do sth
"She stopped to buy water." (= paused)		quit / no longer do
"She stopped buying water." (= quit habit)
rememberdon't forget (future/pending action)
"Remember to lock the door." (= don't forget)		recall a past action
"I remember locking the door." (= I recall doing it)
forgetnot remember to do (action not done)
"I forgot to post the letter." (= I didn't post it)		not recall doing (action was done)
"I'll never forget meeting you." (= I always remember it)
trymake an effort / attempt
"He tried to lift the box." (= made effort)		experiment / test as solution
"Try adding some salt." (= see if it helps)
regretsorry to say sth now (formal)
"We regret to inform you…" (present announcement)		sorry about a past action
"I regret saying that." (= sorry I said it)
go onmove to a new / different activity
"He went on to become a doctor."		continue the same activity
"She went on talking for an hour."
meanintend to do
"I didn't mean to hurt you."		involve / result in
"This means working overtime."
🧠 Memory tip for stop/remember/forget: If the action comes after the stopping/remembering → to-inf. If the action happened before → V-ing.

Pattern: Bare Infinitive

The bare infinitive is the base form of the verb without 'to'.

1. Modal verbs + bare infinitive

can / could / will / would / may / might / shall / should / must + bare inf
"She can speak French."   "You should see a doctor."
cancouldwillwouldmaymightshallshouldmust

2. make / let / have + object + bare inf

"She made him clean his room." (no 'to'!)
"Let her speak."   "I had the technician fix it."

3. would rather / had better + bare inf

"I would rather stay home tonight." (not 'to stay')
"You had better see a doctor." (not 'to see')
❌ Common error: "You had better to go." ✗   "I'd rather to stay." ✗

4. Perception verbs + bare inf vs. V-ing

see / hear / watch / feel + obj + bare inf (complete action)
see / hear / watch / feel + obj + V-ing (action in progress)
"I saw her cross the road." (= the whole action)
"I saw her crossing the road." (= in the middle of it)

Perfect & Passive Infinitive / Gerund

1. Perfect infinitive: to have + past participle

Use when the action happened before the main verb time:
"She seems to have left already." (she left before now)
"He is said to have been a great artist." (past reference)

2. Perfect gerund: having + past participle

Same idea, after verbs/prepositions that take -ing:
"Having finished the work, she went home."
"He regrets having told her the truth."

3. Passive infinitive: to be + past participle

Subject receives the action:
"She wants to be promoted."
"The project needs to be completed by Friday."

4. Passive gerund: being + past participle

After verbs/prepositions that take -ing:
"She hates being interrupted during meetings."
"I remember being told this story as a child."
🧠 Summary: Perfect = action before main time. Passive = subject is acted upon. Combine: "She seems to have been told." = Perfect + Passive infinitive.
